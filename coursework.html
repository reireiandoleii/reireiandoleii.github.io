HTML Lists

Starting strong with this activity! This was our introduction to HTML lists, and honestly, it was pretty fun. We were tasked with organizing information using different types of lists, including ordered, unordered, and nested lists. Since this was one of our first activities, everything was still relatively easy to understand. I enjoyed how simple it was to arrange information and make everything look organized without needing anything too complicated. It was also satisfying to see how a few lines of code could create something presentable. Overall, this activity gave me a pretty good first impression of HTML.

HTML Tables

This activity introduced us to HTML tables, using what I would consider the barebones, old-fashioned way of making a webpage without CSS. We had to organize different programming languages, their uses, and other information into a table. It was actually pretty enjoyable, especially since I was starting to understand how HTML elements worked together. Although the final result looked pretty basic, it made me appreciate CSS a lot more because of how much it could improve a webpage's appearance. For once, I was genuinely excited to finish an activity, and I was pretty satisfied with how everything turned out.

CSS Student Profile

Okay, this was genuinely one of the activities I enjoyed the most! And I wonder why? Because it's CSS! I absolutely love styling and making things look presentable, so creating this student profile was pretty fun. What I didn't enjoy, however, was having less than 30 minutes to finish it. Sure, that's probably possible, but we were JUST introduced to CSS! Of course I'd still be figuring things out and asking questions like a complete beginner. Nevertheless, I enjoyed this activity and wished we had more time to explore everything. Unfortunately, things only started getting more difficult from here.

Interactive Color Palette

Unfortunately, things started going downhill from here. This activity involved making an interactive color palette that changes the appearance of a poster using JavaScript. I initially thought I understood what we were doing, but the more complicated the activities became, the less confident I felt. Surprisingly, I managed to understand most of this one, except for that one black border that absolutely refused to cooperate. Some of the methods we were taught also felt unnecessarily complicated, which made everything more confusing than it needed to be. Nevertheless, I still managed to learn something, even if the process was frustrating.

JavaScript Ticket Calculator

Don't even get me started on this activity. We were tasked with creating a ticket calculator using JavaScript, and I was genuinely confused throughout most of it. I had to study the lesson on my own, but even after sitting down and trying to understand everything, I still struggled. I only managed to complete around 40% of the activity independently. What surprised me the most was discovering that JavaScript could actually perform mathematical calculations, including addition, subtraction, and discounts. I genuinely had no idea we could do that yet! Although this activity was frustrating, it showed me how much more I needed to learn.
